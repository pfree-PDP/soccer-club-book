# Structural Map v1: The Soccer Club Management Handbook

*Based on Stage 1 Discovery Session*

## The Core Concept
The book is a practical, experience-driven handbook for the accidental club leader. The foundational premise is that **every team is a small business**, and most people who volunteer to manage a team or join a board do not know what they are walking into. The book serves as the orientation manual, warning label, and operating guide that should have existed before they said yes.

## The Structural Framework: A Journey in Stages
The book follows a single, cumulative journey rather than splitting into parallel tracks for different audiences. The reader grows, and the book grows with them. The author's personal arc — from AYSO volunteer to co-executive director — serves as the credibility thread that anchors each stage.

### Stage 0: Before You Know What You're Getting Into
- **The Core Problem:** The knowledge gap at the point of entry.
- **The Parent Dynamic:** Parents volunteer because they care, which naturally includes a desire for control. This is not a flaw, but it creates the central tension of club management.
- **The Paradigm Shift:** A team is not just a group of kids playing soccer; it is a small business with expenses, administration, and real stakes.

### Stage 1: The Startup Phase (One Team)
- **The Reality:** You are the operator. You manage the players, the parents, the schedule, and the money.
- **The Challenges:** Basic operations, setting expectations, handling initial parent dynamics.
- **Author's Anchor:** The early days as a referee and first-time coach.

### Stage 2: Early Growth (Multiple Teams)
- **The Transition:** Moving from managing your own team to coordinating groups of teams.
- **The Operational Reality:** The shift from doing the work to building the systems that let others do the work (registration, field logistics, coach recruitment).
- **The Hard Truth:** You are no longer managing players; you are managing coaches of varying skill levels.
- **Author's Anchor:** Co-leading 19 teams and 250+ players with Dave Wilcox.

### Stage 3: Formalization (Joining or Building a Real Club)
- **The Recognition:** Acknowledging the limits of what volunteer effort alone can sustain.
- **Governance:** The transition to a formal board structure.
- **The Episodic Board Problem:** The danger of constant board turnover, loss of institutional memory, and the "soccer team communication" analogy.
- **Author's Anchor:** Merging into Santa Barbara Soccer Club and the first three years on the board.

### Stage 4: Crisis and Survival
- **The Stress Test:** What happens when the informal systems break under pressure.
- **Financial Oversight:** The danger of entrepreneurial independence — how untracked decisions and budget blindness lead to cash flow crises.
- **The Survival Mandate:** Knowing what financial resources exist (e.g., government programs) and how to access them.
- **Author's Anchor:** Navigating the COVID financial crisis and keeping the club alive.

### Stage 5: Standardizing (Club 2.0)
- **The Pivot:** Moving from chaotic and reactive to standardized and proactive.
- **The Unicorn Problem:** Why you cannot hire one executive director to do everything; designing the job before you hire for it.
- **Building Infrastructure:** Establishing coach pay levels, formalizing roles, and implementing real accounting practices.
- **The Hard Truth:** The business needs someone who knows how to run a business.
- **Author's Anchor:** The Club 2.0 restructuring as co-executive director with Sherlyn Voltes.

### Stage 6: Optimizing (Club 3.0)
- **The Evolution:** Going from standardizing (systems) to optimizing (excellence).
- **The Stakes:** As professionalism ratchets up, so do expectations and costs. Managing the shift from "fun" to "pathway to college/pro."
- **Culture:** Why culture and alignment cannot emerge on their own and must be deliberately built.
- **Author's Anchor:** The transition to elite programming and the author's current focus on culture and craft as a high school coach.

---

## Editorial Principles Established
1. **Handbook, Not Memoir:** The author's story is the doorway; the practical guidance is the room. The ratio leans heavily toward *what you need to know* over *what happened to me*.
2. **Volunteer Ethos:** The author did all of this as a volunteer, not for a salary. This establishes a unique moral authority.
3. **Honest Observation:** The book will name the uncomfortable truths (e.g., parent control dynamics, the executive director unicorn, the episodic board) that most resources ignore.
